This is a guide, not a rulebook. Every colour in your palette can be worn anywhere: clothing, makeup, nail polish, jewellery, eyewear, ties, hats, scarves and beyond. Use this as a reference to understand what works with your natural colouring. Colour analysis is about empowerment, not restriction.

The Warm Spring palette contains thousands of colours. The swatches here are carefully chosen examples of what to look for: warm undertone, medium to medium-high intensity, light to medium value. Your colours are fresh and golden, with a tropical kind of warmth. Use these as a visual compass, not a checklist.

Screen tip: For the most accurate colour reference, turn off your blue light filter and set your screen brightness to around 80 percent or higher. On iPhone look for Night Shift under Settings > Display & Brightness. On Samsung look for Eye Comfort Shield under Settings > Display. Samsung screens also show colour more accurately with Screen mode set to Natural rather than Vivid.

Online shopping tip: When unsure about a colour, try asking an AI assistant: "Is [Brand] [Product] in [Colour Name] cool, warm, or neutral toned? Does it suit Warm Spring?" Keep in mind AI pulls from across the internet and isn't always accurate, since sources can conflict. Always check samples in store when possible.

Accessories & Eyewear
The bigger picture: Every colour in your palette can be referenced for accessories: jewellery, bags, shoes, scarves, eyewear, ties, hats, nail polish, and more. A caramel bag, a coral scarf, a watermelon nail, it all works.

Gold is your match, and a rich 18 to 24 karat yellow gold looks especially good. Brass, rose gold, and soft or brushed copper work too, and mixing warm metals is completely fine. Polished and shiny finishes suit you as well as brushed ones. Avoid: bright, icy silver and platinum.

Light tortoiseshell, caramel, and honey brown frames look natural on you. A teal frame or a gold wire frame is a fun option too. Avoid: stark black or bright silver frames.
Lens tints for sunglasses
Amber, honey brown, or bronze lenses are your easiest options. A light peach tint works well too. Avoid grey, blue, or silver mirrored lenses, which can read harsher than your natural colouring.
Bags & Shoes

Caramel
Butterscotch
Maple

Caramel and butterscotch are your most useful bag and shoe colours, with maple and milk chocolate for darker pieces. A vanilla bag is lovely for summer, and a coral bag or sandal is a fun change from brown. Avoid: stark black and cool grey, which read heavier than anything in your natural palette.
Scarves, Hats & Wraps
Near-face accessories matter most
Anything worn close to your face has the same impact as a top or blouse. A coral scarf, a straw hat in biscuit, or a turquoise wrap near your face will all look great. Tip: a tropical print mixing two or three of your colours, like clementine, kiwi, and teal, is an easy way to pull an outfit together.
Patterns & Fabrics
Some ideas to look for
Fresh and playful, with a slightly toasted, golden warmth. Think tropical leaves, flowing florals, gingham, and soft organic shapes. Linen, cotton, and silk with a gentle sheen or light texture all feel right. These are just a few ideas. If a pattern is made up of your colours, it's worth trying.

Coral, watermelon, and cherry tomato are all stunning. For a lighter nail, try melon or a sheer peach. A grape like Concord is a fun change in the fall. Avoid: icy pinks, cool berries, and blue-based reds.
For the Men
Ties, pocket squares & accessories
The same palette applies. A tropical teal or coral tie is a classic, versatile choice. Caramel, oat, and sailor blue suits are strong bases. Kiwi and clementine make fun pocket squares if you want a little more personality. Gold cufflinks over bright silver. A coconut cream shirt over stark white. A cerulean or cherry tomato tie over anything icy grey.

Foundation & Tinted Moisturiser
Foundation rule: warm or golden undertone.
What to look for
This applies across every skin tone and depth. Look for shades described as warm, golden, peach, or yellow-based. Avoid anything described as pink or cool. Tip: swatch on your jawline in natural light. A match that blends seamlessly is right; one that pulls noticeably pink or grey is off.
Eyes
Your eyes look great in golden browns, peachy tones, and warm greens and teals. A soft eye works beautifully with a stronger lip, and a stronger eye pairs well with a quieter lip.
Eyeshadow: what works
Base eyeshadow: coconut cream, vanilla, shortbread, peach. Depth: caramel, honey brown, maple, milk chocolate. Accents: butternut, kiwi, caribbean, kingfisher. Golden and peachy shimmers look especially good. Avoid: cool grey, icy silver, and cool lavender.
Eyeliner
Warm brown, teal, or a soft navy are your most versatile liners, each giving a different look. Tip: a brown liner with a coral lip is an easy everyday look.
Mascara
Brown mascara, from soft brown to dark brown, is the most harmonious.

Hair Colour
Your most beautiful hair colour is often the one you were born with. Hair in this placement leans warm, which is where there's the most harmony.
Your best hair colours
Your naturals can range from a level 4, sometimes up to a 7. Typically warm-leaning browns and reds, from light brown through dark brown, plus chestnut, auburn, and copper.
What to ask your colourist
Warm tones like rich browns, chestnut, auburn, and copper are all harmonious in this placement. Whatever look you're going for, staying within a level of your natural colour is always the most harmonious. You can also bring your palette to show your colourist.
